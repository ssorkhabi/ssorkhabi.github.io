## Mountains as natural laboratories
{: .rsrcht}

The study of altitude adaptation in montane species presents an opportunity to address critical questions in biology, including whether or not evolution is repeatable, and whether or not wild populations can keep pace and adapt to a rapidly changing climate.
{: .research}

**Repeatability of evolution**
{: .research}

If the history of life were repeated, would it produce the same outcomes? Gould famously considered this question in his “replaying the tape of life” thought experiment, rather pessimistically concluding historical contingency would cause each replay to follow a different evolutionary trajectory. Yet observations in nature show similar environments can repeatedly produce similar phenotypes and, in some cases, recruit the same genes and genetic pathways. Studying repeatability of adaptation to altitude in Andean butterflies that diverged ~80 million years ago can provide insights!
{: .research}

**Understanding responses to climate change**
{: .research}

How relevant are mountain ranges when studying population persistence under climate change? Mountains compress environmental variation into short geographic distances, allowing adaptation across divergent environments to be studied at tractable spatial scales and, importantly, often in the presence of ongoing gene flow between markedly different habitats. But is the connection straightforward?
{: .research}

**Sorkhabi, S.**, Montejo-Kovacevich, G., Elias, M., Jiggins, C. D., North, H. L. (2026). [What, if anything, can studies of adaptation to altitude reveal about responses to climate change?](https://doi.org/10.32942/X2HH5B){:target="_blank"} _EcoEvoRxiv_.
{: .research}

## The role of sexual antagonism in wing shape evolution
{: .rsrcht}

Males and Females occupy different reproductive roles, and their success is determined by different, often conflicting selection pressures. How does this conflict manifest in the evolution of wing shape in _D. melanogaster_?
